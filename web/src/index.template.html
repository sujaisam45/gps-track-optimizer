How the cleaning works

  1. Check the fixes

    Drop fixes with no position or timestamp, duplicates, and single jumps faster than 4 m/s, which are usually signal reflections.

  2. Work in metres

    Convert latitude and longitude to the local UTM grid, so every tolerance is a real distance on the ground.

  3. Merge stops

    A run of fixes is a stop when all of them stay within the stop radius for the shortest-stop time and the walker drifts slower than the allowed speed. The run becomes one fix at its median position, keeping its start and end time. Speed alone is not enough: noise makes a standing person look like they move, and careful walking can be slow.

  4. Simplify straight stretches

    Douglas–Peucker joins the ends of a stretch and keeps the fix furthest from that line if it is beyond the tolerance, then repeats on both halves. A turn is always the furthest fix of its stretch, so turns survive; fixes along a straight street do not.

Limits

  • The demo walks are simulated around Place du Vieux-Marché in Rouen, with a known true path. They are not real recordings. The street layout is mostly straight, so real routes with curves keep more fixes.
  • With phone-grade GPS (about 3 m of error) stops are found unreliably and no simplification can recover the true path. The method is meant for RTK receivers.
  • A receiver that is consistently offset to one side stays offset. Cleaning removes noise, not bias.
  • Default settings have not yet been tuned on real RTK traces.
